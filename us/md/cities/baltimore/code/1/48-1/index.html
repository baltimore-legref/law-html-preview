Baltimore City Code

§ 48-1. Definitions.

(a) In general.

In this subtitle, the following terms have the meanings indicated.

(b) Community benefit agreement.

“Community benefit agreement” means a legally binding negotiated agreement between a grant recipient and a community association that serves the neighborhood where the grant recipient is located.

(c) Community-based enterprise.

“Community-based enterprise” means a business that:

(1) distributes or re-invests any profits or financial surplus from the business for the benefit of the community as a whole; and

(2) is controlled in some way by the community where it is located, including:

(i) majority representation on the board of directors by community members;

(ii) democratic decision-making by members of the community regarding the practices of the enterprise; or

(iii) complete or majority ownership by the community as a whole.

(d) Community food retailer.

“Community food retailer” means a retail goods establishment whose primary business is selling food to the general public for off-premises consumption that:

(1) dedicates at least 25% of its floor area to the sale of fresh produce, meats, and dairy products;

(2) aggregates, stores, and distributes locally or regionally produced food to consumers or retail establishments;

(3) is a food cooperative organized under State law;

(4) is a mobile food vendor that operates on a regular schedule in 1 or more healthy food priority areas in the City; or

(5) is a farmers’ market operating under a City permit.

(e) Culturally relevant.

“Culturally relevant” means that a food product reflects the dietary traditions and preferences of the local community where it is sold.

(f) Diet-related illness.

“Diet-related illness” means a health condition caused by, aggravated by, or otherwise connected to diet, including:

(1) coronary heart disease;

(2) obesity;

(3) high blood pressure; and

(4) diabetes.

(g) Division.

“Division” means the Food Policy and Planning Division within the Department of Planning.

(h) Food Policy Director.

“Food Policy Director” means the Director of the Food Policy and Planning Division within the Department of Planning.

(i) Grocery store.

“Grocery store” means a retail goods establishment:

(1) whose primary business is selling food to the general public for off-premises consumption;

(2) that derives at least 20% of its gross receipts from the retail sale of fresh produce, meats, and dairy products; and

(3) that has a floor area of more than 12,000 square feet.

(j) Healthy foods grant.

“Healthy foods grant” means a grant awarded pursuant to this subtitle.

(k) Healthy food priority area.

“Healthy food priority area” means an area in which:

(1) the distance to a supermarket is more than 1/4 mile;

(2) the median household income is at or below 185% of the federal poverty level, as measured by the most recent 5-year estimate of the United States Census Bureau’s American Community Survey;

(3) over 30% of households have no vehicle available, as measured by the most recent 5-year estimate of the United States Census Bureau’s American Community Survey; and

(4) the Healthy Food Availability Index average score of all food stores is low.

(l) Program.

“Program” means the Healthy Food Priority Area Grant Program established under § 48-6 {“Healthy Food Priority Area Grant Program”} of this subtitle.

(m) Retail goods establishment.

“Retail goods establishment” has the meaning stated in City Code Article 32, § 1-312 {“‘Property line’ to ‘Roof deck’”}.

(n) Small box grocery store.

“Small box grocery store” means a grocery store that has a floor area of less than 12,000 square feet.

(o) SNAP.

“SNAP” means the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program administered by the United States Department of Agriculture.

(p) Technical assistance.

“Technical assistance” means operational support services provided to a grant recipient to promote the success of a healthy food retail business, including:

(1) business planning;

(2) financial management;

(3) supply chain optimization;

(4) healthy food merchandising; and

(5) SNAP and WIC technology integration.

(q) WIC.

“WIC” means the Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants, and Children administered by the United States Department of Agriculture.

(r) Worker-owned cooperative.

“Worker-owned cooperative” means a business that is owned and managed exclusively by its employees.

(Ord. 26-147 Section 1.)
